## The three pillars, argued out

### Pillar 1 — "Will this hurt my OF?" → No, and here's why

- **Nothing moves.** She doesn't delete anything, repost anything, or change her OF posting schedule. OF stays the content vault; TXT is a conversation lane that sits next to it.
- **Different spend occasion.** OF money happens in browse mode — fan opens the app, scrolls, maybe buys. Text money happens in attention mode — her name lights up his phone between work emails. Those are different wallets in the fan's head. One doesn't drain the other.
- **It captures money OF already lost.** Expired subs and fans who bounced off the paywall are earning her $0 on OF today. Every dollar they spend on TXT is recovered, not diverted.
- **Her OF stats stay intact.** She's not posting less, not redirecting her feed traffic away from OF, not violating anything by having a business line elsewhere. Rank, earnings history, sub count — untouched.
- The honest framing of the whole product: **TXT is where her DMs should have lived all along.** OF is the gallery; the thread is the relationship.

### Pillar 2 — The $200 cap costs her the whales

- This business is whale math: a handful of top spenders drive most of the revenue. OF caps every paid message at $200, so her biggest fans **literally cannot spend what they want to spend**.
- The mechanics of the loss: a $1,500 custom on OF has to be chopped into eight $200 messages. Every chunk is a fresh decision point for the fan to bail — momentum usually dies by chunk three. Or she doesn't attempt the sale at all, which is worse.
- On TXT the per-message max is $9,999: one offer, one Apple Pay tap. Customs, bundles, girlfriend-experience packages, VIP tiers — priced like what they are instead of folded into $200 installments.
- Second-order effect: when big-ticket offers are *possible*, the whole menu ladders up. A whale who paid $800 once is a different customer than one who's been trained that nothing costs more than $200.
- One-liner for the pitch: **"OF caps your best fan at $200 a message. Your line goes to $9,999."**

### Pillar 3 — Apple Pay

- The single biggest leak in an OF funnel is the card form. Typing 16 digits kills impulse; declines kill momentum; and plenty of fans simply won't put a card into an adult site at all.
- On TXT the fan pays the way he pays for everything else on his phone: **double-click, Face ID, done — without ever leaving the thread.** The purchase happens at the exact second of peak intent, not 90 seconds and one checkout page later.
- Fewer declines, no re-entering expired cards, no "I'll do it later" (he never does).
- One-liner: **"On OF, the fan has to go find his wallet. On TXT, his wallet is his face."**

| "Who answers the texts?" | Whatever arrangement we agree on — you, you with our team's support, or managed with your sign-off. We set the ground rules with you up front, and we don't run plays you haven't approved. |
| "What's the cut / how do payouts work?" | [Confirm with TXT: payout %, schedule, method. Do not improvise this one — it's the question they'll verify.] |
| "Nobody's heard of TXT." | The fan never experiences "TXT" — he experiences texting you. There's no app for him to trust or download. The platform is invisible to the person paying. |
| "I don't have time for another platform." | That's the point of the format — it's your Messages-style thread, not a feed to maintain. [X] minutes a day, and ALTR handles launch, menu, and promo assets. |
| "What about chargebacks / payment risk?" | Apple Pay transactions carry verified payment credentials — decline and fraud rates are a fraction of typed-card checkouts. [Confirm TXT's chargeback policy before quoting it.] |
