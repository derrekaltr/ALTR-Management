# TXT.com Migration & SMS Marketing Playbook

A playbook for moving a creator's fanbase from OnlyFans to TXT.com, where fans text a real phone number. Everything here is built on honest framing: it's the creator's business line ("my line," "my private number"), run by her or a disclosed team per platform norms. The pitch is the *experience* — texting instead of a platform inbox — not a false claim about who's typing. That version sells fine, and it doesn't collapse the first time a fan screenshots a conversation.

---

## 1. Migration campaign: moving fans from OF to TXT

### The announcement arc (run over 7–10 days on OF + socials)

**Beat 1 — Tease (days 1–3).** Don't name the platform yet. Story posts and feed captions: "Something big is changing about how you can reach me." Poll stickers: "Would you rather DM me on an app or just... text me?" This builds curiosity and warms the audience for a behavior change.

**Beat 2 — Reveal (day 4).** Announce the move with the one-line pitch: *"You can text me now. A real number, in your real messages app. No app to open, no feed to scroll."* Show a screenshot-style graphic of an iMessage thread (mocked up, clearly hers). Drop the tap-to-text link.

**Beat 3 — Countdown + founding offer (days 5–8).** "First 100 people who text get [perk]" — a free unlock, a voice-note hello, a locked-in rate. Number the perk honestly and actually cap it. Daily countdown stickers.

**Beat 4 — Last call (days 9–10).** "I'm winding down here. If you want to keep talking to me, my line is the place." Pin the link everywhere.

### Direct outreach to existing subs

- **Mass DM to active subs** with the tap-to-text link and the founding offer. Keep it short — three sentences max. The link should open their Messages app pre-filled (see §2).
- **Win-back message to expired subs.** This is a separate list and a separate message: "I left OF / I'm mostly off OF now. No subscription — just text me." Subscription fatigue is why most of them churned; a no-subscription text line is the pitch that wins them back.
- **Segment the whales.** Top spenders get a personal-feeling message (even if templated): "You were one of my favorites over there — I want you on my line first."

### Angles that make the migration itself the content

- **The "real customs" angle.** OF caps paid messages at $200, which made serious customs impossible to sell in one piece. "The stuff I could never do properly on OF — full customs, big bundles — my line can actually sell" is both an announcement and an offer aimed straight at her top spenders.
- **The friction angle.** "No login. No app. No algorithm deciding if you see me. Just a text thread that's always at the top of your messages."
- **The exclusivity angle (kept honest).** If TXT or the creator genuinely caps how many threads she runs, say so and use it: "I only keep 300 threads open." If there's no real cap, don't invent one — use a true scarcity like limited founding pricing with a real end date.
- **The breakup post.** A "why I'm leaving OnlyFans" post/thread performs well as content in its own right (platform drama travels). It can be honest: fees, the $200-per-message cap, discovery problems.

## 2. Getting the number out

### Make the number one tap away

- **`sms:` links everywhere.** `sms:+15551234567&body=hey%20it's%20me` opens the fan's Messages app with a pre-filled first message. This beats any landing page on mobile. Pre-filling the body also tags the traffic source if you vary the text per platform ("hey, came from your IG").
- **Link-in-bio placement.** The text link goes *first*, above everything else, labeled plainly: "📱 Text me."
- **Keyword funnels on IG/TikTok.** "Comment TEXT and I'll DM you my number" — auto-DM tools handle it. Comment-gating boosts the post in the algorithm *and* captures intent.

### Make the number an artifact fans keep

- **Contact card (.vcf) drop.** A downloadable contact card with her name, photo, and the number. "Add me to your contacts" is a tiny commitment that massively raises the chance they ever text. Her photo then shows up in their Messages app forever.
- **Lockscreen wallpapers with the number baked in.** A free aesthetic wallpaper drop where the number is part of the design. Fans set it, see it daily, share it.
- **QR codes** that encode the `sms:` link — on story graphics, pinned posts, prints, polaroids shipped with merch, convention cards. A QR on a physical item ("text me when it arrives") converts shipping into a conversation starter.
- **Vanity/memorable number** if TXT supports choosing one. A number fans can remember is a number that survives a deleted bookmark.

### Platform-by-platform funnel notes

- **Instagram:** keyword auto-DMs, story link stickers, highlight reel titled "TEXT ME" with a how-to. Keep the promo SFW; IG nukes accounts for adult solicitation, so the IG layer sells the *texting experience*, not explicit content.
- **TikTok:** strictest platform. Funnel to link-in-bio only; never put the number in captions or on-screen text. "The way to actually talk to me is in my bio."
- **X/Twitter:** the most permissive — the number and `sms:` link can live right in the bio and pinned tweet. Pinned tweet = the iMessage mockup graphic + link.
- **Reddit:** post in the niche subs she's already allowed in, per each sub's self-promo rules; number in profile, not spammed in comments.

### Launch-moment stunts (honest versions)

- **The "save my number" campaign.** A week where every post ends with the same CTA: screenshot the contact card, save it, text "saved" for a free unlock.
- **Number reveal as an event.** Live stream or story sequence revealing the digits one at a time across slides — fans screenshot and assemble it. Gimmicky, shareable, and completely transparent about what it is.
- **Collab cross-drops.** Creators on TXT shout out each other's lines: "my friend just got her line set up, text her and say I sent you" — with the pre-filled body actually saying that, so referrals are measurable.

## 3. Chat strategy: texting is a different medium than OF DMs

> Deep-dive version in **`chat-strategy.md`** — conversation craft, the authenticity architecture, team workflow, and the anti-playbook of OF habits to unlearn. This section stays as the quick reference.

### What changes when it's a real text thread

- **Shorter, burst-style messages.** OF DMs tolerate paragraphs; texting doesn't. Three short messages in a row feel alive; one long block feels like a newsletter.
- **Latency reads as human.** On OF, slow replies feel like being ignored. In texting, a reply 40 minutes later is normal life. Don't burn money chasing instant-response SLAs — a natural rhythm with reliable daily touchpoints outperforms it.
- **The thread is permanent real estate.** Her thread sits in the fan's Messages list between his mom and his boss. One good morning text keeps her at the top of that list all day. That placement is the whole value of the platform — protect it by never spamming it.
- **iMessage features are the moat.** Voice notes, photos that land full-res in the thread, tapback reactions, read receipts. Lean on voice notes especially — a 20-second voice note is cheap to produce and feels 10x more personal than text.

### The welcome sequence (first 3 messages decide everything)

1. **Instant-ish reply** to a new number: warm, short, uses whatever their pre-filled message said. "omg hi 🥹 you actually texted. what should I call you?"
2. **A question, not a pitch.** Get a name and one detail (timezone, how they found her). This is personalization fuel for every future message.
3. **One small, cheap offer within 24h.** A $5–10 unlock as a "welcome to my line" thing. The goal of offer #1 is not revenue — it's teaching the fan that paying in this thread is easy and worth it.

### Pricing and PPV pacing with no $200 ceiling

- **Full price ladder.** $5 → $15 → $35 → $75 → customs into four figures. Texting supports many small asks to build the paying habit, *and* the big single-message asks OF's $200-per-message cap made impossible — a $1,500 custom is one offer and one tap, not eight installments.
- **One offer per "scene."** A conversation arc gets one unlock offer, placed at the natural peak, not three offers machine-gunned in.
- **Tip-jar moments.** Texting makes spontaneous tipping feel natural ("if you loved that voice note…"). Keep a standing low-friction way to tip.
- **Customs as the top tier.** The thread format is perfect for custom requests — the negotiation *is* a conversation.

### Cadence, segments, and re-engagement

- **Daily ritual broadcast** (see §4) to the whole list — this is the heartbeat.
- **Segment by spend:** whales get genuinely more personal attention; window-shoppers get the ritual broadcasts and occasional micro-offers; cold numbers get a re-engagement drip.
- **Re-engagement drip for cold numbers:** day 7 a casual "haven't heard from you, you good?", day 14 a free voice note, day 30 a win-back offer. After that, drop to monthly. Never daily-spam a dead number — it triggers blocks and carrier spam flags.

### Identity framing and trust (the honest version of "finesse")

- **Name the line, not a lie.** "My private line" / "my direct line" is accurate, personal, and exciting. If a disclosed team helps run it, TXT's norms and the creator's own framing should say so somewhere findable (bio, FAQ, first-message footer) — then the day-to-day chat never has to lie.
- **If a fan asks "is this really you?" — answer truthfully.** Whatever the true arrangement is ("yep, it's me," "me and my assistant run this line, the voice notes are all me," etc.), the honest answer, delivered warmly, keeps the customer. A caught lie loses him *and* produces the screenshot that loses the next hundred.
- **Make the real stuff verifiable.** Voice notes, same-day photo proofs ("send me a word and I'll write it on my hand"), occasional short videos. Proof-of-life content is the trust engine of a text-based business — invest in it instead of in deflection scripts.

### Compliance basics (cheap insurance)

- **Consent:** fans texting first is the right pattern — keep it that way. Don't cold-text scraped or purchased numbers (TCPA liability in the US is per-message and brutal).
- **Opt-out:** honor STOP instantly and keep the list clean. Deliverability dies when carriers flag the number as spam, and the number *is* the business.
- **Age-gating and records:** follow whatever verification TXT requires strictly; a text thread is discoverable, so assume every message could be read aloud someday.

## 4. Content types for a text-first funnel

Texting rewards frequent, small, intimate-feeling content over big produced drops:

- **Morning/goodnight texts.** A daily broadcast ritual (selfie + one line, or a voice note). This is the retention engine — it's why the thread stays near the top of their Messages.
- **Voice-note drops.** Weekly "voice memo diary." Audio is the most personal format per minute of effort, and it's inherently proof it's her.
- **Lockscreen/wallpaper drops.** Free ones carry the number (§2); premium ones are a natural $5–10 unlock.
- **Behind-the-scenes micro-clips.** 15–30 seconds, vertical, shot on phone. Low production, high intimacy, perfect for the medium.
- **Low-price unlock library.** A deep catalog of $5–$25 items the chat can pull from contextually, instead of a few $200 bundles.
- **Custom audio** (name drops, personalized voice notes) — high margin, impossible on a feed-based platform, native to this one.
- **Polaroid/print + QR merch.** Physical items that route back to the thread ("text me a pic of where you put it").
- **"Text-to-vote" interactive content.** "A or B for tonight's set?" — replies cost nothing, train the reply habit, and every reply re-opens the conversation window.
- **Event texting.** PPV watch-alongs, birthday messages, "I'm getting ready, want to see the fit?" — timely, synchronous moments that a feed can't do and a text thread does perfectly.
