**Prepared:** July 2026
**Purpose:** Consumer-protection due-diligence summary for anyone considering doing business with Alberto Riehl, DreamSource Consulting, Predictable Premium, or Quantum Leap Consulting.
**Nature of this document:** A compilation of public records, court filings, state licensing data, and published customer complaints. Verified facts and unproven allegations are labeled separately throughout.

## Executive Summary

Alberto Manuel Riehl markets a paid lead-generation program ("Predictable Premium," ~$3,300–$5,800) to licensed life-insurance agents through several company names. The public record shows three categories of concern:

1. **A federal fraud lawsuit** naming him personally, alleging the program is a fraudulent pyramid scheme with an "illusory" money-back guarantee.
2. **A documented, multi-state history of professional license revocations** — insurance licenses revoked in Ohio and Hawaii, plus a canceled Hawaii real-estate license and lapsed California insurance licenses.
3. **A large, consistent body of customer complaints** across independent platforms alleging refund refusal, undelivered services, and aggressive conduct.

He also has a prior business that collapsed amid a bank fraud investigation (per his own published account). Notably, he sells services *to* licensed insurance agents despite having lost his own insurance licenses in multiple states.

## 3. Federal Fraud Lawsuit (VERIFIED — public court record)

**Case:** *Tae Rho v. DreamSource Consulting LLC, et al.*
**Case No.:** 8:23-cv-00705
**Court:** U.S. District Court, Central District of California
**Judge:** David O. Carter (Magistrate: Karen E. Scott)
**Filed:** April 24, 2023

**Named defendants:**
- DreamSource Consulting LLC
- **Alberto Riehl (individually)**
- Predictable Premium R and D LLC
- Doe defendants

**Nature of suit:** "Other Fraud" (personal-property tort), diversity jurisdiction. Filed by the law firm **EKO Law / Erickson Kramer Osborne** (San Francisco) as a proposed class action on behalf of life-insurance agents (initially New York Life agents) who bought the program.

**Core allegations in the complaint:**
- Once agents pay, they find **most or all of the marketing tools violate major carriers' strict advertising rules**, rendering the program unusable.
- The program's true aim is to **convert paying customers into salespeople recruiting more buyers** — described as a **pyramid-scheme structure**.
- The advertised **"100% money-back guarantee" is "illusory"** — refunds are stalled or denied.

**Procedural history:**
- All defendants **served** in May 2023.
- Defendants **failed to respond**; clerk **entered default on July 11, 2023**.
- Plaintiff **voluntarily dismissed the case without prejudice on November 24, 2023.**

**Honest characterization of this outcome:** "Without prejudice" means the case was dropped, **not decided** — there was **no court finding of liability**, and the claim can be refiled. A voluntary dismissal following a default is commonly consistent with a private settlement (often confidential), but that is *not confirmed* on the public docket. What is verified: a federal fraud complaint was filed naming Riehl personally, the defendants defaulted rather than defend, and the matter ended by voluntary dismissal.
