# Rubric 02 — Peptides, GLP-1s, and Regulated Goods

Peptide accounts sit on the worst possible policy square: Meta's **Restricted Goods and
Services** standard treats attempts to buy, sell, trade, or *solicit* pharmaceutical
drugs as removable (Layer 1, strikes), and health/wellness compound content is
independently non-recommendable (Layer 2) even when purely educational. Meta ran
aggressive 2025–2026 enforcement waves against GLP-1 and research-peptide sellers,
including ad-account and profile disables — this niche gets less benefit of the doubt
than any other we handle.

**2026 legal context (tell peptide clients this — it's bigger than Instagram):**
- The FDA formally killed the "research use only" loophole: in March–April 2026 it
  issued warning-letter waves to telehealth compounders and online peptide sellers,
  explicitly disregarding RUO/"not for human consumption" disclaimers when marketing
  described weight loss or performance effects.
- The FTC is enforcing directly against **fitness influencers on Instagram/TikTok**:
  unsubstantiated before/after peptide claims and undisclosed vendor affiliations
  (codes, kickbacks) are active FTC targets, coordinated with state AGs.
- So a peptide client's flagged account is often the smallest of their problems: the
  same content is federal-enforcement evidence. This strengthens the case for the
  Tier 2 pivot below.

## The bright line
**Educational content about a compound: allowed but mostly non-recommendable.**
**Anything that helps a viewer obtain the compound: violation.**
"Helps obtain" is read broadly and includes:
- Price mentions, discount codes, "link in bio," "DM for sourcing," "my supplier"
- Linking (even one hop via Linktree) to a site that sells peptides, "research
  chemicals," or gray-market GLP-1s
- "For research purposes only" disclaimers — Meta explicitly ignores these; they are
  treated as evidence of intent to sell, not protection
- Affiliate relationships with peptide vendors, including untagged ones the audit can
  infer from recurring vendor mentions

## Audit checklist
- Bio/link: any route to a vendor site? (Follow the link chain one hop.)
- Compound naming with sourcing context: BPC-157, TB-500, semaglutide/tirzepatide,
  ipamorelin, CJC-1295, "sema," "tirz," "research peps" — the compound name alone is
  a Layer 2 signal; name + any obtain-path is Layer 1
- Dosing protocols presented as instructions ("start at 250mcg twice daily") —
  removable as facilitating non-prescribed pharmaceutical use, and separately flagged
  as health misinformation risk
- Before/after transformations attributed to a compound (health-claim + promotion combo)
- Vials, syringes, reconstitution videos on camera — strong visual classifier signal
  even with no caption at all
- Comment sections: is the creator answering "where do you get yours?" — replies count
  as solicitation
- Story highlights named after compounds or "protocols"

## Severity mapping
- Any obtain-path (link, price, code, DM CTA, vendor tag) → **CRITICAL** — this is the
  content getting the account disabled, not just demoted
- Dosing instructions, injection/vial visuals, compound-named highlights → **HIGH**
- Educational compound discussion, before/afters, transformation claims → **MEDIUM**
  (allowed, but building account-level non-recommendability with every post)
- General biohacking/longevity talk without named compounds → **LOW**

## Fix tiers

**Tier 1 — Adjust:**
- Strip every obtain-path: remove codes, prices, vendor links (including from the
  Linktree), "DM me" CTAs, and stop answering sourcing questions in comments (use
  Hidden Words to auto-filter "source"/"where to buy" comments so replies aren't
  tempted).
- Reframe dosing content as "what the research literature reports" citing studies,
  not as instructions. Remove second-person imperatives ("you should take…").
- Take vials/syringes out of frame; talk over B-roll of training, bloodwork
  paperwork, whiteboards.

**Tier 2 — Rework (the durable play for this niche):**
- Pivot the account's public content one level up the funnel: recovery science,
  longevity, bloodwork literacy, training, sleep, nutrition — recommendable topics
  that reach the same audience. Compound-specific depth moves OFF Instagram to a
  newsletter, podcast, or site (promoted generically: "full breakdown in this week's
  newsletter" with no compound name in the IG post).
- If the client sells: selling peptides through Instagram is not fixable with
  phrasing — no wording makes it compliant. Commerce must live entirely off-platform
  with no Instagram breadcrumb to it. If they're licensed telehealth, they need to go
  through Meta's LegitScript-style certification path for ads instead of organic
  workarounds.

**Tier 3 — Delete/archive:**
- Everything CRITICAL, immediately — each one is standing strike inventory.
- Compound-named highlights and reconstitution/injection tutorials.
- If the account already has restricted reach: archive all HIGH+ compound content,
  run 4–6 weeks of the reworked content mix, monitor Account Status weekly.
