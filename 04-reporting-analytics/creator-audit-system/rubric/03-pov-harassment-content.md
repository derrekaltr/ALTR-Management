# Rubric 03 — POV / Smart-Glasses Street Content (Harassment-Pattern)

The niche: first-person (Meta Ray-Ban / smart-glasses or bodycam-style) footage of
approaching strangers in public — pranks, confrontations, pickup content, "social
experiments," rating/mocking people, filming reactions. This content trips **Bullying
and Harassment** (Layer 1) and the recommendation exclusion for **content depicting
people being mocked, pranked, or harassed without consent** (Layer 2). It also
generates the enforcement signal platforms weight heaviest of all: **victim and
bystander reports**, which trigger human review rather than just classifier scoring.

The irony to explain to clients: Meta sells the glasses, but Meta policy doesn't care
what camera shot the footage — it scores what happens in the frame.

## Audit checklist — per video
- Is the filmed person a **non-consenting private individual**? Signals: visible
  discomfort, walking away, saying stop/no, surprise reactions, no cutaway suggesting
  a release was signed, comments tagging/identifying the person
- Does the content's *point* depend on the target's discomfort, embarrassment, or
  anger? (Reaction-bait structure = harassment pattern even if "just a prank")
- Sexualized commentary about or ranking of strangers (overlaps Rubric 01 AND
  harassment — double flag)
- Following someone after disengagement, entering personal space, touching,
  blocking paths — escalates toward removable content and, frankly, real-world
  legal exposure (recording/consent laws vary by state; note this in the report)
- Filming staff/workers who can't leave (retail workers are a recognizable harassment
  category and these clips get mass-reported)
- Minors visible as targets or prominent bystanders → **automatic CRITICAL**, archive
  immediately
- Caption/overlay tone: mocking framing ("watch her face 💀") converts neutral footage
  into a harassment classification

## Account-level signals
- The format IS the account: if >half the grid is non-consensual reaction content,
  assume account-level recommendation ineligibility plus an elevated report-driven
  strike rate. This niche's flags are report-driven, so they arrive in bursts and
  feel "random" to the client — they aren't.

## Severity mapping
- Minors, touching/pursuit, filming visibly distressed people, mocking identifiable
  private individuals → **CRITICAL**
- Non-consensual reaction content as the recurring format; retail-worker content → **HIGH**
- Ambiguous-consent street interviews with neutral tone → **MEDIUM**
- Consented interactions with poor labeling (no indication consent exists) → **LOW**

## Fix tiers

**Tier 1 — Adjust:**
- Blur faces and strip identifying details of anyone who didn't consent — reduces
  report likelihood and drops the "identifiable private individual" element. (Note:
  blurring makes old clips safer to leave up; it does not make new harassment-format
  content recommendable.)
- Cut the mocking captions/overlays; kill comment-section pile-ons on identifiable
  people (or turn off comments on those posts).
- End clips at the interaction, not the reaction — remove the discomfort-as-payoff
  edit structure.

**Tier 2 — Rework (this niche has a proven compliant version):**
- Convert to **consent-forward street content**: ask on camera, show the yes, get a
  quick release (a phone-screen release app takes 20 seconds and creators in the
  street-interview niche use them as standard practice). Street interviews,
  compliments content, paying-strangers formats, skill challenges — same POV
  hardware, same spontaneous energy, fully recommendable.
- Make willing participants the stars: people who enjoy the bit perform better on
  camera anyway, and clips with laughing consenting participants outperform
  discomfort content with the recommendation system actively distributing them.
- Scripted/staged versions with actors for confrontation-style bits, labeled as
  skits — allowed, though "staged prank" labeling honesty matters for monetization.

**Tier 3 — Delete/archive:**
- Everything CRITICAL today — these carry legal exposure, not just platform risk.
- The back catalog of pure reaction-bait: it keeps generating reports as long as it's
  up, and report velocity is what's re-flagging the account "every few days."
- If the client won't change the format: tell them plainly that no edit makes
  non-consensual harassment content sustainable on Instagram — this niche's only
  long-term options are the consent-forward pivot or a different platform's rules
  (which are converging on the same standard anyway).
