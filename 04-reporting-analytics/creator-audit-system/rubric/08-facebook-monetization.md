# Rubric 08 — Facebook Content Monetization
(Core-scope platform. Grounded Oct 2026. Audit lens: WHY content doesn't pay, separated into the two
gates clients conflate.)

## The two gates (diagnose which one is failing)

**Gate 1 — Account/Page eligibility (Partner Monetization Policies):**
invite-only Content Monetization Program (CMP — the single program that
replaced In-Stream Ads, Ads on Reels, and Performance Bonus on Aug 31, 2025);
eligible Page or professional-mode profile; supported country; 18+; no active
policy violations; authentic audience. Status visible to the client in
**Professional Dashboard → Monetization** (eligibility screen names the
specific issue).

**Gate 2 — Per-content eligibility (Content Monetization Policies):**
content can be fully allowed on Facebook and earn $0. The big excluders:
- **Unoriginal content — the #1 cause.** Reposted/third-party footage
  (broadcast clips, game footage, movie/TV scenes, other creators' videos,
  compilations) is ineligible. Meta's Mar 2026 guidance: repeat unoriginal
  posting makes the whole account non-recommendable AND demonetized. Reshared
  content on Stories became ineligible Oct 2026. "Transformation" requires
  substantial added value — real commentary/analysis throughout, not a logo,
  caption, or 5-second intro. Reaction-style frames around someone else's
  footage don't qualify unless the creator's contribution dominates.
- **Rights you don't hold:** sports broadcast footage is licensed property
  (leagues/conferences/networks). Even when a rights-holder doesn't strike the
  post, Meta's Rights Manager matches it and routes/blocks monetization. Sports
  clips are the canonical case: the content plays fine, earns nothing, and
  accumulates originality/IP flags.
- **Engagement bait, clickbait, sensationalism** — ineligible and
  eligibility-threatening.
- **Static/slideshow/low-effort formats** (text-on-image loops, stock +
  AI-voiceover) — "limited originality" treatment. AI-assisted is fine ONLY
  when the human authorship and added value are real.
- **Restricted categories** mirror the usual list (gambling, sexual
  suggestiveness, drugs, violence, misinformation) — ad-unsuitable even when
  the content stays up.

## Audit procedure
1. Classify the last 15–20 posts: original footage vs. third-party footage vs.
   transformed. For sports pages: who shot it? Broadcast angles, scorebugs,
   network graphics = third-party, full stop.
2. Look for the transformation test: is there creator-authored commentary that
   dominates, or just clips + caption?
3. Check format mix (Reels vs. long video vs. images) — CMP pays on content
   types; image-meme pages earn near nothing regardless.
4. Funnel/baits: "follow for more," tag-bait captions.
5. Verdict names the failing gate explicitly and gives the compliant pivot:
   original commentary/analysis formats (face + voice on camera), licensed or
   self-shot footage, creator-owned formats (rankings, predictions, debates,
   interviews) — the ESPN-clip page → sports-talk creator conversion.

## What the client must pull
Professional Dashboard → Monetization (eligibility status + any policy issues
listed), Rights Manager/IP notices in Support Inbox, and per-post "not eligible
for monetization" labels in Meta Business Suite.
