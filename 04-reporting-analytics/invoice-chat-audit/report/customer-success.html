2. How the group chats are built

Every client has one Telegram supergroup named <Model> x ALTR. Eric creates it with the ALTR photo, posts the welcome and go-live target, then steps back. The client joins by invite link.

The standing roster in every group is Derrek, Eric, Mayson, Mica, Michelle, Stephski and the assigned chat lead (Fred or Ollie). Since 29 July, Vivek is in every group as the Meta support contact. Larger accounts also carry an account-manager pair over the chat team (Nate and Ronnie). Chatters are never in the client group. Lauchlann, Sofia and Sofia D'Ortone sit in several groups and never post.

The five topics

Telegram forum topics are the whole system. Each function owns one and introduces itself there.

TopicOwnerWhat belongs there
OnlyFansChat leadIntake form, login and handover, go-live, pricing and limits, custom requests, scripts, chatter issues, vault uploads, Snapchat adds, video calls, whale handling.

The general thread is where things get lost. On the worst accounts most of the conversation lives there: Sarah Lloyd 76%, Kaitlynn 71%, Vicky 66%, Amber 48%, Haileigh 43%, Lauryn 41%. Sarah Lloyd's group was created without an OnlyFans topic, so every chat-team issue runs in general. Vicky's Invoicing topic was added two weeks late. The clean accounts are the ones where the lead enforces topics: Lilia 12%, Shayna 7%, Mila 4%.

Pinned messages are the only in-chat record: the Drive index, the customs sheet, and on older accounts the team roster. Most groups have nothing pinned.

Routing works by @-mention with a caret: @olkmck ^^, @ofmfrederick ^^. It works when the tagged person is online. It is also the most common way a question dies.

3. What gets sent in each topic

The standard messages, in the order they normally appear. Wording is as used in the chats.

OnlyFans topic (chat lead)

  1. Lead intro. Ollie: "Hey Lauren, I'm Ollie nice to meet you, will be handling your chatting." Fred and Mayson use the longer script: "I handle all things OnlyFans so if you've ever got any questions then pleeeeease do not hesitate to ask!!! I'm always online so I will get a response to you promptly... ALWAYS!!!"
  2. Intake form. "Please fill out the form that will give the team everything they need to know about yourself AND the ability to sell in confidence. Making sure that nothing you DO NOT want sold is ever sold." Google Form, Google Doc or Typeform depending on the lead.
  3. Go-live. "We are starting today at 2pm EST. Was just wondering about snapchat adds? Customs? and Videocalls? If you can let me know pricing." Then: "All in — team will begin now!"
  4. Pricing questions. "What's the minimum price for a custom?" "Are you adding on snapchat and if so how much?"
  5. Custom request. A block headed CUSTOM REQUEST with model, fan, amount paid, duration and details, plus a screenshot of the fan's message. Detailed customs get a Google Doc. Since August the pinned customs Google Sheet: "Here is your custom sheet."
  6. Snapchat add. "He paid for a snapchat add… to be added on snap" with the fan's username. The model adds them herself.
  7. FAQ after a team swap. "Could you fill in this FAQ for us as well please so as a chat team we can have more information on you."
  8. Setup complete (Mayson, day 3 to 17). "We've finished setting up and optimizing your profile. Tracking links are added, promo campaigns for both new and expired subscribers are now running 24/7, automated renewal messages before subscriptions expire and win-back messages after expiry are now active, and your bio/settings have been optimized."
  9. Chatter issue acknowledgements. "Relaying to the team, will add this to the vault notes as well." "I have passed this on and will be in contact with chatters."

Social Media topic (Mica; Mayson for links)

  1. Intro. "I'm Mica, your Social Media Director. I'll be helping you grow your social media pages… IG in addition to TikTok, Snapchat, FB… my booking link to schedule a 1 on 1 call." Calendly, 30 minutes.
  2. Handle intake. "Send me the rest of your social media links… send instagram too even if it's suspended!"
  3. Weekly reel inspiration. "Here's your reel inspo for this week" with eight to ten IG reel links. The same batch goes to every client the same day.
  4. Custom domain (Mayson). "This is a custom domain built with the latest technology that will help you from shadow bans or account restrictions related to your link." One <name>sworld.vip per social account.
  5. IG policy. "If your IG gets suspended, disabled, restricted, or starts acting shadowbanned, please don't submit an appeal right away. Reach out to us first."
  6. Meta-rep handoff. Derrek: "@fonta will assist when he's awake." Vivek: "Sure share login." "They put Facebook under watch till September."

Promo topic (Michelle)

  1. Intro. "I'm Michelle, part of the promo team, we will be handling IG repurposing and promo coordination for your page."
  2. Content request. The <Model> - IG Reels Marketing.pdf: "This is a content request for your management ran Instagram reels… it includes your Google Drive link for uploads, along with a guide and inspo." Drive link re-posted separately.
  3. Requests. "Would you be able to upload more reels please?" "Load a few photos for story post when you get a chance." Roughly weekly.
  4. Go-live. "IG posting is starting this week." Then "This is the ig page we will be posting on." Or: "Backup IG being warmed up, ready next week."
  5. Derrek's 1 August update to every group: Instagram guideline changes, a new phone farm, 200 devices and 1,000 accounts swapped, promo paused until it is running.

6. What works

  • Topic discipline when the lead enforces it. Lilia, Shayna and Mila run at 4 to 12% off-topic and their questions get answered because the owner sees them. Fred's "just responding here so we can keep the conversations on OnlyFans all in place" is the model.
  • Scripted intros with something attached. Every department's intro ends in a form, a booking link or a PDF with a Drive. Clients complete intake forms in 20 to 45 minutes when asked on day 0.
  • Mayson's setup-complete message. A concrete "you are live and optimised" moment that clients respond well to.
  • The customs Google Sheet. Pinned, live, replaces the sprawl of request blocks. Rolled out to five accounts in three weeks.
  • Stephski's cadence. Withdrawal reminder, invoice, three-day follow-up, receipt, every two weeks, every account. Clients pay reliably; the invoicing audit covers the detail.
  • Weekly reel inspiration and the weekly DMCA report. Two cheap, visible touches a week on every account regardless of what else is happening.
  • Vivek. Within ten days of being added he recovered accounts for Sarah H, gave Sarah Lloyd a Facebook status, and took Barbara's Copyright Ban Protection request. First time IG problems had a named owner inside the chat.
  • Leads who front-run problems. Ollie's "just logged into your OF from my phone" before the security email arrives. Fred clearing the auto-scheduler queue and posting the screenshot.
  • Go-live conventions. Scheduled hard start, both pages at once, billing from the go-live date. No client has disputed a start date.
  • Fast median response on most accounts.

7. Gaps across the portfolio

Ranked by how often they show up and how much damage they do.

  1. Nothing enforces the intake form on the chatters. The top complaint on eight of fourteen accounts: chatters selling prohibited content, wrong prices, pre-cutoff content, or implying things the model does not do, after she wrote it all down. Clients end up policing chat themselves. Sarah Lloyd: "makes me feel lowkey crazy having to ask the same thing every day."
  2. The "^^" handoff has no backstop. Mayson tags the owner and moves on. Several of the never-answered threads in section 1 end at a "^^". There is no timer and no escalation.
  3. Questions in the general thread get lost. Six accounts run 40 to 76% of their traffic outside topics. Sarah Lloyd has no OnlyFans topic at all.
  4. Team swaps re-onboard the client at her expense. Fred to Ollie on Vale and Vicky: Drive deleted, April customs never delivered, seven Infloww relinks, FAQ redone, logins drifting between leads. Lilia gave new logins to Mayson and Fred did not know. No handover checklist exists.
  5. Some accounts have no named owner. Lauryn has had nobody in the OF topic since June. Barbara's chat side is invisible. Haileigh asked "Do I have an account manager?" in March. Where Derrek named a POC (Barbara, Mayson) the client still asked "any updates?" and got "Yo Ma'am."
  6. Promises with no tracker. Vicky's traffic. Amber's IG accounts (three built, three banned, disclosed only when she asked; 35 followers months later). Lauren Blake's ads reporting. Barbara's backup IG. The 1 August memo paused promo everywhere with "updates next week."
  7. IG questions go unanswered unless Vivek picks them up. Mila's "my account's been suspended again… so what happens now" (23 Jun) was never answered. Haileigh's shadowban question got "Sure share login" and stopped.
  8. Credentials have no system. Passwords in chat, sometimes at staff request. Pre-group DMs to Derrek nobody else can see. No password manager. Relinks on every change.
  9. Assets live on personal accounts and devices. Drives on individual staff Google accounts, shared albums on personal phones, form responses in the lead's account, credentials in DMs. Anyone new to the account starts blind: "Ah I wasn't on here when it was sent."
  10. Clients get no reporting. The 2025 accounts received a report and a 14-day plan every two weeks. The 2026 accounts get invoices and DMCA screenshots. Clients ask for numbers (Amber, Lauren Blake) and are promised a phone call.
  11. Chatter quality and voice on at least seven accounts: broken English, emoticons the model hates, "are you single" to whales, aggressive tone, telling a fan she has a husband. Fixes are reactive: pay deductions, removing a chatter.
  12. Whale retention after takeover is not measured. Lauren Blake lost 13 of 24 whales (59% of revenue) in the first month. Lilia lost her top spender to a missed flag. Amber's top spender "picked up on" the tone change.
  13. Money friction lands in the CS chat. Four bank or rail changes in 2026, Zelle caps forcing five separate sends, fraud-flagged wires, currency reissues, a premature invoice, off-book payments to Derrek. Stephski absorbs it well; each one still costs trust.
  14. DMCA is a number in a screenshot. The first one arrives unexplained ("What's this?"). High-leak clients say the volume is overwhelming and submit their own links.
