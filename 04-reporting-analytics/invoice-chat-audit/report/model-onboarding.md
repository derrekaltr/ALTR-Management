# ALTR Management — Model Onboarding Process

**Prepared for:** Toto (ecosystem ops)
**Prepared:** August 21, 2026
**Source:** Reconstructed from the 14 `<Model> x ALTR` Telegram team chats (July 2025 – August 8, 2026). Every step below is evidenced by actual chat history; where practice varies between clients, the variants are noted. Client-identifying details, credentials, and banking numbers are excluded.

---

## 1. At a glance

Onboarding runs in **two tracks that overlap**:

1. **Commercial track (off-Telegram, before the group exists):** close the deal, agree the commission, send the contract by email, and in most cases receive OF credentials privately. In several onboardings the chat team was already live on the page *before* the Telegram group was created.
2. **Operational track (in Telegram):** Eric stands up the team group, each department introduces itself in its own topic and drops its intake artifact, account access is transferred, the page goes live, and the recurring cadences (content, socials, promo, DMCA, invoicing) switch on.

**Typical elapsed time from group creation to chatting live: 3 hours – 3 days.** The billing clock always starts at **chat go-live date**, not group creation.

The single most important operational rule observed: **each function owns one Telegram topic and introduces itself there** — OnlyFans, Social Media, Promo, Invoicing, DMCA. Everything routes through topics; cross-posting is corrected by staff ("keeping the conversations on OnlyFans all in place").

## 3. Phase 0 — Before the Telegram group (commercial close)

Conducted off-chat (DMs, calls, email), visible only in retrospect:

- **Deal & commission.** Standard observed structure: **30% month-to-month** with the marketing suite, or **35% including paid marketing**. A separate 5%-of-revenue paid-ads allocation exists on at least one account. Commission disputes were corrected post-hoc twice (35% → 30%, backdated), so the agreed rate must be written down where billing can see it.
- **Contract.** Sent **by email for e-signature** after collecting full name, address, and email. In the best-documented case the client signed within ~16 minutes. **Nuance:** in the 2026 cohort no signature confirmation ever appears in-chat, and one 2025 client had to chase for the contract *after* credentials were already handed over.
- **Pre-group intake.** Some clients complete a "general management" form before the group exists — which later collides with the in-group chatter form ("the one you filled out a few days back was for general management, this one is for the chatters").
- **Early credential handover.** Several clients sent OF/social logins **directly to Derrek by DM** before the group existed; on two accounts chatting was already live before the group was created. This works but leaves the rest of the team unable to see what was handed over (one client's Twitter sat unmanaged for ~10 days because nobody knew who held the login).

## 4. Phase 1 — Telegram group stand-up (Day 0)

Owner: **Eric The Efficient** (2026 standard; Lauren Biz Assistant performed this role in 2025, Derrek occasionally).

Exact sequence, identical across chats:

1. Create group **`<Model> x ALTR`** seeded with the core staff roster (Eric, Derrek, Mayson, Stephski, Michelle, Mica + the assigned chat lead).
2. Set the group photo (branding).
3. Convert to **supergroup**.
4. Create the five standing **topics**: **OnlyFans, Social Media, Promo, Invoicing, DMCA.** (2025 groups had four at creation and retro-fitted Promo/Resources/Facebook; the five-topic set became the day-zero template in 2026.)
5. Client joins **by invite link** (occasionally direct-invited). Staff sometimes greet before the client is even in the room.
6. Eric posts the **welcome + go-live target** — e.g. "We are prepping for a hard start Monday @7pm!" or "We are good to start on your page in an hour!" — and cues the team: "Everyone will introduce themselves here shortly!"

**Watch-outs observed:** one group was created without an OnlyFans topic (OF matters then sprawled across the general thread and amplified escalations); another got its Invoicing topic 14 days late; one whole group had to be re-created because the original wasn't a supergroup and couldn't hold topics. The five-topic checklist matters.

## 5. Phase 2 — Department introductions & intake (Day 0–2)

Each function posts a **scripted intro in its own topic**, each ending in an artifact or booking link:

| Function | Topic | Intro artifact |
|---|---|---|
| OF / chat lead | OnlyFans | **Intake form** — "gives the team everything they need to know about yourself AND the ability to sell in confidence." Tech varies by lead: Google Form, Google Doc, Typeform (2026); Canva site (2025). Collects persona, boundaries, hard limits, per-item pricing. Clients typically complete it in 20–45 minutes. |
| Social Media Director (Mica) | Social Media | Canned intro + **Calendly 30-min 1:1** → Google Meet call within days. |
| Promo (Michelle) | Promo | Canned intro; the reels-marketing PDF + Drive follows within the first week. |
| Invoicing (Stephski) | Invoicing | Introduces **at the first billing cycle**, not day 0. (One premature invoice sent 5 days into an engagement was retracted by Derrek in front of the client — the billing clock starts at go-live and bills on the normal 1st/15th cycle.) |

**The intake form is the highest-leverage and highest-risk artifact.** It is the chatters' selling authority. Two systemic failure modes recur:

1. **Form answers taken too literally** — e.g. a client's "$30/pic" questionnaire answer led chatters to sell full-frontal nudes at $30 when her actual hard line was topless-only. Fix applied on that account: all customs require model approval first (acknowledged trade-off: "it does hurt custom sales massively").
2. **Form answers not propagated at all** — chatters selling content types the form explicitly prohibited ("I said so many times on onboarding forms to not send old tapes"). This is the single most common source of week-1 escalations.

Supplementary intake observed on various accounts: a spender/"endorsement" briefing on named whales, chatter persona/tone PDFs, video-call and Snapchat pricing Q&A in-topic, and (2025) a scripted **voice-note batch** (~100 lines read verbatim from a PDF) as the first client deliverable before any content request.

## 6. Phase 3 — Account access handover (the critical path)

Three mechanisms observed, in decreasing order of cleanliness:

1. **CRM-to-CRM handover (cleanest).** The outgoing agency disconnects from Infloww/OnlyMonster at an agreed time (e.g. midnight UK); ALTR connects its own **Infloww** immediately after. No password drama. Requires the client to coordinate the old team's logout — chase this actively.
2. **Infloww face-auth deep links.** Chat lead sends `login.infloww.com` plus an `inflowwlogin://…face-authentication-start/…` deep link; the model taps it on her phone and passes OF Face ID. Works, but **every password change forces a fresh relink** (one account needed 7 relinks in 5 weeks), captchas intermittently block logins, and a client seat invite can collide with the model's own existing Infloww org.
3. **Password handover.** The model changes her password and delivers it. Best observed practice: **DM to one designated staff member** ("pls dm your logins to @… instead of here on the gc… just to keep it safe"). Worst but frequent practice: **plaintext in the group chat** — including one case where staff explicitly told the client to post it there. Also seen: relaying live 2FA codes from the outgoing agency's authenticator with a 30-second window, and disabling OF 2FA entirely to restore team access.

**Go-live conventions:** house default is a scheduled "hard start" (00:00 BST or 8pm EST are the recurring anchors); if the client has **two pages, both start simultaneously** ("there are fans that are on both accounts"); clients migrating from another agency may negotiate a 1-day stagger so the old agency can close out its final invoice. The chat lead confirms in-topic: "All in — team will begin now."

**Post-login guardrails** (from the Instagram-takeover cases): treat a fresh login as fragile — 24-hour no-change freeze after a suspicious-login flag, no forced logout of other sessions, no bio/settings changes on day 1.

## 7. Phase 4 — Content pipeline

- **Primary channel: Google Drive**, one folder per purpose — **OF content and promo reels are always separate drives.** Best-practice observed: a pinned "G-DRIVE QUICK ACCESS" index (General / Additional SFW / Additional NSFW / OF Feed posts / Scripts) posted in week 1. Content managers pull from Drive into the **OF vault**, handle previews and classification; the model just uploads.
- **The fallback ladder** (invented under pressure, now effectively standard): Drive → send in Telegram *as file* (compression warning) → **iCloud shared album** via a personal phone number → direct-to-vault or a dedicated OF uploader account. Roughly half of clients ended up on a fallback; Drive literacy and phone-storage issues are the usual cause. Expect to teach Drive basics step-by-step.
- **Content Snare** (the 2025 submission portal with bi-weekly deadlines) was **abandoned in Feb 2026** after client resistance ("You guys get zero content from me because it's a pain") — approved by Eric, replaced with plain Drive folders. Old Snare links kept being issued for weeks after; make sure the whole team knows the current channel per client.
- **Supporting artifacts** issued by the chat lead as needed: sexting-script PDFs (`NewFansScript…`), voice-note scripts, live-stream game PDFs, OF bio drafts run as **1-week A/B tests**, vault-format rules (mp4).
- **Customs tracking** matured over time: ad-hoc "CUSTOM REQUEST" templates → a Notion board (killed when Notion removed the explicit-content board) → the current **pinned Google Sheets customs tracker**, rolled out across accounts in July–August 2026.

## 8. Phase 5 — Social & link infrastructure

- **Handles intake** happens on/around Mica's 1:1 call: IG (all accounts, "even if suspended"), TikTok, Snapchat, FB, X. Link-page photos (PG-to-bikini; no lingerie) are collected — sometimes by SMS because Telegram compresses.
- **Link-in-bio evolution:** juicy.bio → link.me → **custom `<name>sworld.vip` domains** (Mayson, standard from June 2026; up to 7 per client, one per social account, sometimes specced to mirror the client's existing link page). Links go in the **IG bio only — never TikTok**. Domains still get flagged occasionally; Mica may order a "hold off on adding it" pause.
- **Managed/backup IG accounts:** created on the phone farm, **~7-day warm-up** before posting, then management-run reels accounts post from the promo Drive (benchmark quota: **35 reels/week**; in-person "content days" bank 100–200 reels in ~3 hours). The promo team disclose the handles once live. Facebook: created under the **stage name**, grown to ~5k, then taken over by management; Meta Business access via tutorial video + business ID.
- **Standing cadences:** Mica's weekly reel-inspo drop (8–10 links, same batch across clients); Michelle's reel-upload chases; the "trial reels" method (3/day) on the model's own accounts.
- **IG incident policy** (formalized June 2026): *never self-appeal a suspension — contact ALTR first.* Vendor unbans are fronted by ALTR and re-billed at cost on the next invoice. **Vivek (@fonta), Meta Support Rep**, was added to every group on 2026-07-29 as the standing escalation channel.

## 9. Phase 6 — Billing onboarding

- **Cadence:** biweekly, invoices on the **1st and 15th**, periods 1–14 / 15–EOM. First invoice period starts at **chat go-live date**. Payment expected within 5–7 days; pre-invoice reminders ask the client to start her OF withdrawal early ("we stop messaging that after the first time you're on time").
- **Invoice format:** PDF named `<Client legal name> <period>.pdf`, wire/Zelle details at the bottom. FX handling for non-USD clients (GBP invoices reissued on request; conversion provided by Alex Camus).
- **Chargebacks are netted before invoicing** ("we are not invoicing on earnings not earned") — the standard answer to invoice-vs-dashboard discrepancy questions.
- **Known friction to design around** (full detail in the separate invoicing audit): repeated payment-rail changes forced re-notification of every client; Zelle daily caps force split payments; international wires get fraud-flagged; crypto is accepted case-by-case (get the chain right); one client habitually paid Derrek directly, bypassing reconciliation. New-rail hygiene that worked: clients running $10/$100 **test transfers** before paying in full.

## 10. Phase 7 — DMCA / leak protection

- Started **automatically off information already on file** — the client does not need to supply links. A **baseline leak scan** is run (Google index, piracy collections, Reddit, X), and the client is asked to **approve/extend the "searched names" list**.
- Cadence: scans/takedowns daily, **weekly reports** posted in the DMCA topic (historically Wednesdays; formats evolved from per-source link counts to cumulative "Total Infringements Removed" screenshots). Ownership moved Nick|DMCA → "Updates" bot → **Michelle Queue** (current).
- **Nuance:** the first weekly report is often the client's first contact with the function ("What's this?") — a one-line explanation at intro time prevents that. Clients can and do submit their own found links in-topic.

## 12. Nuances & failure modes worth designing out

Ranked by how often they burned an onboarding:

1. **Intake data doesn't reach the chatters.** Boundary and pricing violations in week 1 are the #1 escalation across chats. The form exists; the enforcement loop doesn't. A per-client "hard limits" card surfaced inside the chatting CRM, plus customs-approval defaults, would close most of it.
2. **Credential hygiene is inconsistent.** Everything from a proper DM-to-designated-staff rule to passwords pasted in group chat (sometimes at staff request), 2FA removed entirely, and live code relays. Pick one standard (DM to a named credential holder + password manager) and enforce it.
3. **Team swaps destroy onboarding artifacts.** When a chat team was replaced, the old team's Drive was deleted (pre-switch customs never delivered), the FAQ had to be re-collected from scratch, and logins drifted without the new lead knowing. There is no handover protocol; every swap re-runs a mini-onboarding at the client's expense.
4. **The contract step is invisible post-2025.** No in-chat signature confirmations in the 2026 cohort; one client chased for her contract after credentials were live; commission terms had to be corrected retroactively twice. A signed-contract + agreed-rate checkpoint before go-live would prevent both.
5. **Single point of contact is ambiguous.** One client asked "Do I have an account manager?" nine months in, and the staffer had to go find out. Where Derrek explicitly named a POC ("@mmayson is your go to for all things ALTR") the account ran noticeably smoother.
6. **Drives are scattered and permissioned wrong.** View-only upload folders, invites that never arrive, clients finding links "in another chat," folders accidentally deleted by staff. One canonical, pinned drive index per client (the Quick Access pattern) fixes this.
7. **Pre-group handovers leave the team blind.** When credentials go to Derrek before the group exists, the rest of the team can't see what ALTR actually holds (the unmanaged-Twitter case). Log every credential received pre-group into the group's pinned message or the intake doc.
8. **Duplicate/lost intake asks.** Clients filled two overlapping forms days apart, or filled the form and nobody looked ("Oh weird I did fill it out"). One form, one owner, one confirmation.
9. **Topic discipline slips at creation.** Missing OnlyFans or Invoicing topics measurably worsened the noise on those accounts. The five-topic set is a checklist item, not a convention.
10. **Promises made at close need a delivery tracker.** Migration-cohort clients repeatedly escalated on the gap between the sales pitch and week-3 reality (socials lagging, no reporting). The 2025 cohort's 30-day report + 14-day game-plan cadence handled expectations far better than the 2026 cohort's ad-hoc updates — worth reviving.
