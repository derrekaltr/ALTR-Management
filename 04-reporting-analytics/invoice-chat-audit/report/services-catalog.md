## 1. The menu at a glance

ALTR sells **one bundled management retainer** (rev-share, invoiced biweekly) that wraps ~8 core services, plus a small set of **priced add-ons**, a large family of **fan-side monetization services** (the actual revenue engine — agency sells to fans on the client's behalf and takes its % via the invoice), and many **free value-adds** used for retention. Line-item pricing almost never appears in chat — the only explicit rate card ever shown to a client is Sarah's contract pitch:

> "30% month to month with a suite of marketing… OR 35% month to month with all 30% inclusions but adding paid marketing" (Sarah #25, Sep 2025)

**Successfully sold (money changed hands / billed):** the core 30% retainer ×14 clients; the +5% paid-ads uplift (Liza, Lauren Blake); IG unban fees (Sarah, $2,275 billed); every fan-side custom/call/live/Snapchat-add brokered through chat.

## 4. What the data says about your selling

1. **You sell one thing well: the bundle.** 14/14 clients on the retainer, and the fan-side monetization machine inside it is prolific (hundreds of brokered customs/calls/adds; a $50 tape doing $10k day one). The bundle's stickiest, most-praised component is DMCA takedowns — it appears in every chat and never gets complaints.
2. **Discrete paid upsells almost never close.** Across ~13 months the only stand-alone paid add-ons that ever converted were the +5% ads uplift (×2) and Sarah's IG unban fees (×1 clean, ×1 murky). Everything else — Copyright Ban Protection, cost-split Meta rep, third-party recovery, proposals — was offered without a price and closed at zero. Pattern: **unpriced offers don't convert.** The one client who asked what the cost-split would actually cost got silence.
3. **Sold-but-unproven is your recurring exposure.** Every percentage upsell that closed generated an accountability demand you didn't meet in writing: Liza asked twice for ads statistics (promised "full summary," never sent), Lauren Blake asked for Meta spend reports (deflected to a call), Amber asked for ad screenshots (never shown). Same shape as the December unban round, where the client recovered her own accounts while the paid rep "worked." If you keep selling percentage/fee add-ons, attach a report artifact to them.
4. **Free value-adds are your best retention tools, and the cheapest ones worked best.** The trial-reels tutorial (a forwarded video) got explicit praise and adoption; the reports/game-plans were loved and then silently discontinued when Raw Agency exited — more than one client's engagement visibly cooled after. The Wildthing yoga cross-promo converted zero.
5. **The referral program is quietly your best acquisition channel** — Vale's $5,720 credit almost certainly bought you Shayna, and she was referring another prospect two days later — but its terms exist nowhere in writing in any chat.
6. **Declines are respected, which protects the relationships** (H's Facebook decline, Li's wall-posting decline, S's 35% pass) — but there's no re-pitch motion either: the 35% tier was never offered to anyone again, and dropped inquiries (Fansly, H's SoCal collabs, B's collab reel) just evaporate. There is no visible follow-up system for open offers.
